Valfodr

Valfodr is a downloadable content superboss in Final Fantasy XIII-2 in the match "No Mercy for the Judged", a part of Snow's Story: Perpetual Battlefield scenario. He drops various crystals for some of the unique Paradigm Pack monsters which cannot otherwise be reacquired.

Valfodr increases in level after every fight. Locking the Coliseum -??? AF- gate in the Historia Crux will reset his level to 1, allowing the player to repeat the lower-level fights at their discretion.

Valfodr at levels 70 and 99 and Gilgamesh are the only enemies with a triple-digit enemy points value.

Battle[]

Valfodr attacks with his axe and gun-arm, and summons reinforcements. The axe hits in small area in front of him. The gun hits five times, dealing half of the axe's damage in total. At level 15 he gains another, stronger gun attack which hits 27 times in an arc, dealing 50% more damage per hit than the basic gun attack. For clarity, the gun attacks are labeled Gun A and Gun B, respectively.

Valfodr uses Divine abilities to make either one or both weapons glow, permanently increasing their power and Cut value. The axe deals 50% more damage, Gun A deals 250% damage 10 times and gun B deals double damage. Abilities are unaffected.

Level 1[]

At start of the battle and after every 60 seconds, Valfodr summons three Leyaks into battle to assist, up to a maximum of five. Leyaks bestow Bravery, Faith, Protect, Shell and Veil on him before physically attacking the party.

Valfodr casts Fire twice two or three times in a row, then Fira, then gun A once or twice two times in a row. After falling below 40% HP, he attacks one to three times with three axe blows, then with Gun A once or twice, then casts Fira.

Strategy[]

Valfodr's weakest form is easy to defeat, as his attacks do not inflict a great deal of damage for a well-developed party. The player shouldn't require the services of a Sentinel. If the player is fighting this battle directly after Snow, they may want to instantly retry the battle if they wish to tweak their paradigm deck.

The player should be able to defeat the Leyaks quickly enough before Valfodr's chain gauge resets. Once they are defeated, the player should shift to a paradigm containing two Saboteurs and a monster Synergist to remove Valfodr's buffs, and apply some debuffs, such as Deprotect, Deshell and Imperil, while the Synergist applies Bravery, Faith and Enfire to the party. With these in place, Valfodr should fall quickly.

Level 15[]

At start of the battle and after every 180 seconds (210 below 50% HP), Valfodr summons two Golden Chocobos, up to a maximum of two. The chocobos can inflict Daze, Deprotect and Deshell with Supersonic Wave.

Valfodr uses Gun A twice one or two times, casts Thunder twice two or three times, attacks with his axe, casts Thundara, then attacks twice with his axe. When his HP drops below 50%, he uses Divine Trigger to empower his gun. After that, he attacks four times with a one or two of the following: Gun A, Gun B and Thundaga. He then attacks with his axe once or twice.

Strategy[]

Whenever Golden Chocobos are summoned, the player should aim to defeat them as quickly as possible. Once Valfodr is on his own, the player can start inflicting debuffs and applying buffs as in the previous battle.

Even after Valfodr becomes stronger after his HP is halved, the player may be able to get away without using a Sentinel, although that means the player will have to heal more often.

Level 45[]

When the battle begins, Valfodr immediately uses Divine Blade to empower his axe. He attacks three or four times with one to three axe blows, Gun A, Firas and Thundaras in any combination.

Immediately after falling below 80% HP and every 50 seconds afterwards, Valfodr will summon three Cactuaramas, up to a maximum of four. The Cactuaramas cure his status ailments and restore his HP at an alarming rate. He occasionally casts Faithga to improve the Cactuaramas' healing abilities.

After falling below 55% HP, Valfodr starts using Bolverk which deals massive, physical damage (six times his strength) in a line and may also inflict Daze for 5 seconds. After Bolverk, he uses three to four attacks combining one or two of the following: two axe attacks; gun B; two casts of Fira or Thundara; Painga; Fogga; Bolverk if below 25% HP. He can use Bolverk multiple times in a row.

Strategy[]

The player must attempt to inflict more damage to Valfodr than the Cactuaramas can heal. The most efficient way to defeat Valfodr is to utilize a monster Synergist capable of casting Enfire, and possibly Bravega and/or Faithga. Cactuaramas are weak against fire and they can be defeated quickly if the player changes to an offensive Wide-tuned paradigm, like Cerberus-W (COM/COM/COM), as soon as they spawn. Thus the player can spend longer periods battling Valfodr on his own until he summons more reinforcements.

The player should avoid using a Sentinel and utilize paradigms such as Diversity-W to top up HP, and also use Noel or Serah as a Synergist from time to time to bestow Protect and Shell.

Level 70[]

When the battle begins, Valfodr immediately uses Divine Radiance to empower his axe and gun. He attacks three times combining one or two of the following: two axe attacks, Gun A or B and Thundaga. After four combos, he uses Bolverk.

Immediately after falling below 80% HP and every 30 seconds afterwards, Valfodr will summon three Chichus into battle, up to a maximum of five. After that, he only uses Firaga, Deprotega or Bravega, which augments the Chichus' rapid attacks.

After falling below 50% HP, Valfodr's eyes will begin glowing. At this point he uses Bolverk, performs four attack combos, then casts Deprotega twice. Occasionally, he will cast Bravega. The attack combos are two or three attacks (or Bolverk) that combine one or two of the following: one to three axe blows, Gun A or B, Firaga or Thundaga. As before, he can use Bolverk multiple times in a row.

Below 20% Hp, the first Bolverk is replaced with Valfodr's signature move, Gagnrath - a defense-piercing, two-part, physical attack which deals extreme damage in front of him. At first, pillars of light deal damage three times and launch his target before he follows up with a blow from his axe, dealing ten times his strength in damage and a third in wound damage.

Strategy[]

The player should use a monster Synergist to cast buffs onto the party, particularly an En-type buff, as Chichu are weak against all elements. If the player uses Wide-tuned offensive paradigms whenever Chichus are summoned, particularly in combination with an En-type buff and either Bravery or Faith, the Chichus should not last long, allowing the player to concentrate on Valfodr. A Medic should be deployed to deal with Valfodr's Deprotega, and casting Veil will help as well.

The player must be ready to shift to Tortoise to survive Gagnrath.

Level 99[]

When the battle begins, Valfodr immediately uses Divine Awakening to empower himself. In addition to his weapons, his entire body will glow. He no longer summons anything.

Valfodr casts Ultima which is defense-piercing but has a 98% damage variance - it can deal between 140 and 6,700 damage. After Ultima, he attacks twice with Gun A or B twice in a row, Cursega and Slowga (sometimes multiple times), two or three axe blows twice in a row, Firaga or Thundaga followed by two Gun A, and finally Bolverk.

After falling below 60% HP, Valfodr's eyes will begin glowing. He uses Gagnrath, then buffs himself with Haste, Bravery and Faith. He follows up with a new ability, Quasar, which deals six hits with a small radius, inflicts wound damage and launches. After Quasar, he attacks once or twice with either four axe blows or three Gun B, then attacks twice with his axe and follows up with Bolverk. Afterwards, Valfodr can cast Deprotega and Cursega, possibly multiple times like before, or skip them entirely.

Strategy[]

The player should bring a healthy stock of Wound Potions.

The player should defend with Tortoise against Valfodr's Ultima. As in previous battles, the player should start by casting both offensive and defensive buffs on the party, and debuff Valfodr with Deprotect, Deshell, and Imperil (Valfodr is slightly easier to debuff than in the previous battle), but this time it may be wise to deploy a Sentinel for much of the battle, as Valfodr's standard attacks are more powerful than before.

Valfodr is difficult to damage heavily most of the time, so the player must take advantage of the stagger period when it arrives. Using monsters with Feral Links that increase chain bonuses significantly when staggered (such as Lightning and Sazh) work well, as does the Cerberus paradigm, but the player should only deploy Cerberus when the party is in healthy condition and use it in small doses, otherwise Valfodr will decimate the party.

During the second stage the player must dispel Valfodr's buffs immediately, otherwise his attacks will overwhelm the party. The player should use Tortoise whenever the prompt for Gagnrath and Quasar appears. Other than that, the player should continue employing the same strategy as in the first stage.

Valfodr never heals his HP, and there is no time limit, so the player may wish to use a powerful monster Sentinel (Pulse Gladiator and Snow are the two best Sentinels in the game) for the whole fight, and cast defensive buffs, particularly Vigilance, so that wound damage is kept in check, and use a defensive strategy to gradually whittle Valfodr's HP down. However, such a strategy may take hours.

Paradigm Pack[]

While Valfodr can be tamed by defeating him at any level, he will most likely relinquish his crystal once defeated at level 99. A Ravager, his initial stats are impressive, and with the right infusing methods they rise much beyond that as he levels up. Valfodr is useful when facing enemies with an elemental weakness or inflicted with Imperil, as he is the only monster that learns all the Fel-type abilities, as well as Weak Spot. Due to those unique trademarks, his damage output is comparable to that of COMs. His spellcasting is also incredibly fast. Valfodr is the most powerful Ravager, with 6 ATB, respectable HP with a Mana-focused build, and the highest Magic in the game.

His main fault is that he is expensive to develop—he requires 269 crystals to reach level 99. As defeating Valfodr at his most powerful is one of the biggest challenges in Final Fantasy XIII-2, if the player only obtains his crystal from Valfodr Lv. 99, there may be no other challenges on offer for the player to utilize him.

Etymology[]

Valfodr's name as well as two of his attacks, Bolverk and Gagnrath, are alternative names Odin has assumed in Norse mythology. Gagnrath, correctly translated as Gagnrad, appears as a weapon available to Lightning in Lightning Returns: Final Fantasy XIII.
